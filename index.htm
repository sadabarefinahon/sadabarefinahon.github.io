01

What actually pulls me in

I'm drawn to tech as a system, not tech as a feature. The part that keeps me up is the connective tissue — how one layer of a machine hands off to the next, how a program is actually built underneath the interface, why an algorithm behaves the way it does once real data hits it.

That curiosity is why CSE felt inevitable rather than chosen. I'm one semester into the degree at Southeast University, and I'm treating this stretch as the point where the hobbies step aside and the real building starts.

03

The long game

Whatever I end up building, it has to clear three bars at once.

Solves something that matters

Not a convenience feature — a real, major problem worth the effort.

Affordable, or genuinely top-tier

No comfortable middle. It either reaches everyone, or it pushes the ceiling of what's possible.

Built to outlast me

A generational legacy — something still standing, and still mattering, long after.

Direct neural-code connection

A direct interface between human thought and written code, cutting out the translation layer between intention and instruction.

Biological data center

Storage and computation grown or run on biological substrates instead of pure silicon.

Subconscious background processing

Offloading routine cognitive work to run quietly in the background of the mind, the way a computer runs a background process.

Bio-digital firewall

A defense layer at the boundary where biological systems and digital systems start to merge.
